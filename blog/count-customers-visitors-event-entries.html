Events and business

How to Count Customers, Visitors or Event Entries

A simple tally can estimate foot traffic when each entry has a clear counting rule.

Agree on what counts

Decide whether you are counting entries, unique visitors, or transactions. These are different measures. For example, if a visitor leaves and returns, decide in advance whether that is one visitor or two entries. Make sure everyone helping with the count follows the same rule.

Choose a counting position

Stand where the flow is visible and safe, without blocking the entrance. Keep the counter ready and tap once for each event that meets your rule. If several entrances are in use, assign a counter to each or use a documented method to combine totals.

Make a simple event plan

Choose a time window, position the counter where entries can be seen clearly, and agree on how to handle groups, children, staff, and re-entry. For long events, note the count at regular intervals or at shift changes so a handoff does not lose the running total.

Record context with the result

At the end, note the date, time window, location, and counting rule alongside the total. A tally app is useful for a quick manual count, but it does not identify people or replace required ticketing and access-control systems.

Report estimates honestly

A manual tally can miss people when the entrance is busy or several doors are open. Describe the result as an estimate if that is what the method provides, and record the collection window and counting conditions. Avoid collecting personal details unless you have a valid reason and follow the event's privacy requirements. For ticket validation or occupancy limits, use the approved access-control system instead of a basic counter.

Clear rules make a small tally more useful and easier for another person to understand.
