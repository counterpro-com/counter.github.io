Daily tracking

How to Track Water Intake, Pages Read, or Tasks Completed

A simple tally works best for a repeatable action with a clear unit.

Choose one unit per counter

For water, decide what one count means, such as one glass or one bottle. For reading, count pages or completed chapters, not both in the same total. For tasks, define what “complete” means before you start. Clear units make your final number meaningful.

Update the count as you go

Tap after the action is complete and keep the phone somewhere convenient. Review your total at a natural point in the day. Avoid setting targets that conflict with your needs; for health-related goals such as hydration, follow advice appropriate to your situation.

Keep context alongside the total

A tally does not automatically explain when an action happened or what you counted. Write down useful notes or use a dedicated tracking app if you need timestamps, reminders, or history. See how a habit counter can support routines.

Make each tally easy to interpret

Write the unit in the counter label or in a nearby note: “250 ml cups,” “pages,” or “completed tasks.” If your cup or bottle size varies, a count alone will not represent a precise volume. For reading, decide whether you count pages started or pages finished. For task lists, count only items that meet your completion rule.

Review without turning it into pressure

Look at totals as a prompt to notice patterns, not as a measure of personal worth. Adjust the routine if it creates stress or conflicts with other needs. A simple counter usually cannot provide context, times, or reliable health advice; use an appropriate tracker or professional guidance when the task requires more than a basic count.
